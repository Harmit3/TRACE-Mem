# Independent validation resource estimate

**Planning only. No new inference ran.** The proposed target is 90 families and 360 histories. The common two-call primary condition has 1,440 policy-history episodes and a ceiling of 2,880 logical verifier calls. Each mode over zero/two/five calls has `360 × 4 × (0+2+5) = 10,080` maximum logical verifier calls and 4,320 one-question reader evaluations. Both modes give ceilings of 20,160 verifier calls and 8,640 reader evaluations. Optional forecasts at half and double horizon are separate secondary runs. Scheduling those only for three paced policies at the two-call ceiling adds up to 4,320 verifier calls and 2,160 reader evaluations. Budget approval must include which optional runs are scheduled.

Step 30 measured 814 fresh verifier requests, 233,459 input and 9,537 output tokens, and 730.4 seconds summed verifier latency. This gives about 287 input tokens, 12 output tokens and 0.90 seconds per fresh call on that host. The short reader measured 29 fresh calls, 11,308 input and 1,025 output tokens and 71.3 seconds, or about 390 input tokens, 35 output tokens and 2.46 seconds per fresh call. Those reader tasks were small. Step 32 retrieval-backed reader time was 62.6 seconds for 24 fresh calls, about 2.61 seconds per call. Both models ran locally at USD 0. Step 30 observed 4,129 MiB of GPU use and about 997 MiB peak runner working set.

Using the Step 30 per-call mean and no cache reuse, the two-mode scope would take about **5.0 hours verifier time plus 5.9 hours short reader time**, before processing overhead or exceptional timeouts. The estimate corresponds to about 5.8 million verifier input tokens and 236,000 output tokens, and 3.4 million reader input tokens and 305,000 output tokens. The two-call primary alone is roughly 43 minutes of verifier time plus 59 minutes of reader time, under the same assumptions. Actual eligibility and exact cache reuse may reduce these totals. Step 30 reused 15,675/16,489 logical verifier requests, and Step 31 reused all 20,450. Fresh independently authored mechanisms may have far fewer repeats, so these rates should not be used as a commitment. Reserve at least the cold-cache allowance and use sequential checkpointed operation.

Use local pinned Qwen, MiniLM and DeBERTa only after baseline adapters, safety controls, hardware and budgets have been approved. No paid service is required by design. Raw safety and reviewer data remain local. The largest remaining resource is qualified independent fixture and protocol review for 90 genuinely distinct families. No reviewer time, permission, participation or successful replication is assumed.
