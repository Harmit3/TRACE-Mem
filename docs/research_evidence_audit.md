# Research evidence audit

This consolidation covers the tracked repository through Step 33, the experiment log, freeze manifests, matching saved local artifacts, and the implemented temporal replay and evaluation paths. Origin main was at `540a5a1`, the Step 33 merge. The audit made no model calls, touched no new benchmark items, and retained all earlier results.

## Evidence hierarchy

- **Software verification.** Replay ordering, schema validation, budget ceilings, leakage guards, deletion projection and cache invalidation tests support specified implementation invariants. They provide neither effect estimates nor external performance evidence. Earlier smoke and provider checks are explicitly classified in the inventory.
- **Training, calibration and held-out synthetic analysis.** Step 17 generated 200 fictional histories in 40 design families, with 100/50/50 histories and 20/10/10 groups across train/calibration/evaluation. Step 18 fit L2 logistic regression with class balancing, grouped folds and train-only scaling (seed 23). Fit/calibration/evaluation used 112/63/37 labeled candidates. The held-out evaluation includes six labeled positives in four families and 37 labeled candidates in five families overall. The evaluation partition is independent of model fitting, but cases share family templates and the label depends on a frozen verifier intervention. AP .849 versus .877 for no NLI and a paired full-minus-ablated interval spanning zero support exploratory ranking, with calibration and NLI benefits unresolved.
- **Synthetic policy evaluation.** Step 30 uses matched, source-authored candidate histories and historical-read and current-slot checks. Step 31 returns to the same histories to examine robustness. This design isolates allocation from real extraction quality and cannot answer the end-to-end claim.
- **Development and human-assisted studies.** Steps 19–29 reuse prefixes or conditions on one reserved Memora development persona. The 18 human approval events fell between Steps 27 and 29 and were a review workflow. The 15 questions and 2,400 Step 24 rows do not constitute 2,400 independent evaluations. Adapted local FAMA is suitable for failure isolation. The Step 29 allows/restrictions were human-approved with AI assistance, and the recovered index constrains serving to that reviewed scope. Step 27's autonomous resolver released zero real targets. Zero leakage in tested grants and queries is a limited enforcement result rather than evidence of autonomous resolution or broad privacy safety.
- **Independent external diagnostic.** Step 32 samples 14 cleaned LongMemEval questions by metadata before inference. Transcript and oracle arms completed 14 each under a local judge. Only one history passed extraction completeness and authorization for managed replay. All arms missed the answer on that common history. The external evaluation is evidence of coverage and reliability bottlenecks, with no defensible estimated controller advantage. Step 33 used new fictional fixtures and preserved the examined external cohort and its six pending human-review cases.

## Why the replay comparison reverses

At 250 calls each, Step 30 gave T2 vs H-paced 341/600 vs 409/600 on the fixed stream and 434/600 vs 394/600 in chronological replay. T2's paired difference moves from −11.3 points [−16.3, −6.3] to +6.7 [0.7, 12.3]. Fixed selection freezes B2 proposals and targets before replay. Accepted corrections can change the validity or identity of later targets. Calls on those stale targets still consume their budget without applying the verdict. At 50%, T2 had 94 stale calls and H-paced 70, with 75 vs 99 helpful applied outcomes. Chronological policies form targets from live state, with zero stale targets for these runs. At the nominal horizon, T2 spent earlier on average than H-paced (positions 2.56 vs 3.96 of five), made 122 vs 85 helpful applied calls, and adopted 12 vs 60 harmful proposals. These co-occurring mechanisms explain why an offline ranking comparison and a deployment-style replay answer different questions. The study did not intervene on staleness in isolation.

B3 often matches or beats T2. At 100% horizon and 50% allowance, T2's +3.7-point estimate versus B3 has a paired interval [−1.7, +9.0]. At doubled horizon, it drops to 59.0% vs 68.7% using 211 vs 250 calls, a −9.7-point difference with interval [−16.3, −3.2]. At the 100% allowance and doubled horizon it uses 295/500 calls and trails by 21.7 points. At nominal horizon and 100%, both reach 598/600. Closed-loop accuracy gains over H-paced are conditional on horizon and budget. T2-no-NLI loses 4.2 points to full T2 at nominal 50% with 250 calls each. That contrast includes score and reference distribution differences. The score-by-pacing interaction interval crosses zero.

Memory accuracy is six exact checks per history, comprising retrospective as-of and final-state checks. It is not a question-answer score. On the Step 31 reader subset, T2 answers 7/30 short questions correctly, B3 6/30 and H-paced 12/30. This small subset provides no positive QA transfer estimate. The original README's broad H1 requires independent replication, H2 lacks end-to-end support, and H3 is inconclusive because there is no isolated temporal or provenance removal study with independent QA outcomes. The proposed manuscript question is retrospective framing. Step 30's own committed plan and Step 31's post-hoc protocol keep their original status.

## Independent statistical checks

The audit script reads each checkpoint and checks its exact content against the archived report. It reconstructs 100 Step 30 and 130 Step 31 condition rows and tests stored state counts, history counts, cap, spending, staleness and authorizations. It separately rebuilds 40 and 144 paired intervals using Python's seeded draws and a hand-computed linear percentile. Each history is paired within the same family. Before computing a Step 30 B4 history score, it averages all five predefined seeds, 7, 23, 41, 61 and 83. Each 95% interval uses 2,000 resamples of the ten family means with seed 9001. Neither audit is an independent replication of model responses.

There are **ten design-family clusters with ten histories each** in the principal analysis. Those clusters are the resampling units. Authored update archetypes recur even across domains, so independence between families is an assumption of the descriptive analysis rather than a demonstrated population property. The datasets supply 100 histories and 500 B2 reference conflict opportunities. The 600 current-state checks per condition, 500 historical checks per condition, multiple seeds or horizons, 28,000 Step 30 and 26,000 Step 31 deletion checks, and 184 reported intervals do not add independent units. The primary interval estimates condition on the trained scores, the observed families and verifier. They omit model refit, dataset shift and multiplicity. Do not report corrected significance or confuse the two reused benchmark-development question sets with independently sampled deployments.

The Step 30 fixed stream verifies exactly the frozen quota for verification-enabled policies. Closed loop caps calls at the allowance, with nominal per-cohort amounts 0/50/125/250/500. The audit sums 16,489 logical Step 30 verifier calls over all seeds and conditions, 814 fresh and 15,675 cached. Step 31 logs 20,450 logical calls with zero fresh verifier requests and reused the exact Step 30 responses. Its logical token allocation associates old response/attempt rows via the verified single-writer contiguous insertion order, because the old attempt table did not retain request keys. Future ledgers should store keys on attempts and avoid this dependency. Step 32 uses absolute per-history caps of 2 and 5, verified independently of future arrivals. A call cap is not a token or latency cap.

## Consistency and leakage boundaries

- Fixed-prefix V3 compares outcomes conditional on selected, labeled candidates. Pending and unverified cases were not imputed as negative. The v2 log's 113 nonpilot train negatives count candidate rows, while the v2 class-support audit reports 112 negative case IDs. Those are distinct denominators. Do not pool V3 training, calibration, held-out candidates, synthetic Step 30 histories or Memora question–source pairs.
- Historic FAMA summaries variously report scored-only, failure-inclusive and common-question means. They must keep their original denominators. Step 32 recall is post-context trimming and micro over annotated targets, whereas its answer accuracy is per completed question. Oracle abstention has privileged label-derived access.
- The local audit found separation between exported blind inputs and scoring for the checked paths. Dataset revisions and model digests support provenance, but cannot rule out contamination through pretrained model data. Oracle and human-granted evidence must remain labeled. Restricted annotations, raw prompts and answer keys stay off the public release.
- README lists some original work packages as planning items. Prefer the frozen protocols and experiment log for completed work. The published results do not establish hosted-score comparability, calibrated preferences, generalized privacy, or state-of-the-art performance.
- Headline result files and their checked legacy renders match. The checklist enumerates which artifacts were actually regenerated. Earlier engineering milestones with no full machine-readable run, private Step 29 grant and worksheet dependencies, and missing Step 32 rejected response bodies limit exhaustive regeneration. Four original schema failures became valid on replay, so their exact causes remain unknown. Lack of a record is not evidence of a successful run.
